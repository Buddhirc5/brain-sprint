## What the two share (the style to keep)

1. **One accent on neutrals.** A single saturated colour (orange) on charcoal or warm cream. The accent marks the hero object, one key word per line and the active control: nothing else.
2. **A hero object that travels.** An ember or a ball carries the eye from scene to scene and is the cause of every transition: it writes the words, bursts into the reveal, becomes the next shape, fills the frame.
3. **Glow and depth.** A soft light source at the bottom of the frame, bloom on the hero object, and depth-of-field blur on whatever the camera isn't looking at.
4. **Real product pieces, rebuilt big.** The input box, the model menu and the editor are clean rebuilds at about 2× their size. They are never a small screenshot floating in space.
5. **Camera as a character.** It pushes, whips, pulls back to reveal and zooms through objects. Every move carries motion blur.
6. **Kinetic type, word by word.** Thin, calm text with one word in the accent colour, or one word in a bold or underlined form; never full-screen walls of text.
7. **Speed contrast.** Fast whips (0.15–0.3 s) between short holds (0.5–1 s). About a new idea every 1–1.5 s.
8. **Sound: music first.** A modern track carries the film and the cuts sit on its beats. Effects are rare: whooshes on whips, a hit on a landing or a burst, ticks under typing. No narration, at most a 1–2 word spoken tag at the end.
9. **The end.** The brand mark is performed, not faded in, then a one-line claim.
